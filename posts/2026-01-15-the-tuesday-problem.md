---
title: the tuesday problem
date: 2026-01-15
description: why the second day of anything is the one that decides it.
---
everyone plans for monday. nobody plans for tuesday.

monday has a kind of free energy in it. the notebook is new, the folder is empty, the plan still fits on one page. you are not working yet, you are starting, and starting feels like working while costing nothing.

then tuesday arrives and the free energy is gone.

on tuesday the thing is no longer an idea. it is a half-built thing with a shape you did not choose. the first decision has already closed three doors you did not know were open. and the part you were looking forward to turns out to sit behind a part you were not.

i have abandoned a lot of things on a tuesday.

not dramatically. there is never a moment where i decide to stop. i just open something else first, and then the next day the thing has a small layer of dust on it, and the dust is easier to leave than to clear.

what actually separates the things i finished from the things i did not is almost never talent and almost never the idea. it is whether there was something waiting for me on tuesday. a person expecting a draft. a slot in a calendar. a file already open on the screen from the night before.

the trick i keep relearning: end monday in the middle of something. leave a sentence unfinished. leave the test failing. leave one obvious next move sitting there so that tuesday has a handle on it.

a finished day is a closed door. an unfinished day is a rope.

i still forget this most weeks. i still tidy everything up at the end of the day because tidying feels responsible. and then tuesday shows up with nothing to hold on to, and the dust starts.
